Pulmonary embolism and major bleeding during and after primary elective hip and knee replacement in England: national trends and postoperative risk, 2006 to 2024

Question 1 How did pulmonary embolism and major bleeding change nationally from 2006 to 2024?
Question 2 How long did excess risk persist after discharge?
Study design: This population based retrospective cohort included adults undergoing primary elective total knee or hip replacement in England between 1 January 2006 and 30 September 2024. The source was Hospital Episode Statistics Admitted Patient Care, which records inpatient and day-case care provided by NHS hospitals in England together with NHS-commissioned care delivered by independent providers, linked to Office for National Statistics mortality records. Pulmonary embolism comprises qualifying hospital records and linked deaths with a pulmonary embolism code anywhere on the death certificate. Major bleeding comprises qualifying hospital records and linked deaths with a qualifying bleeding code as the underlying cause. In-hospital means the complete admission for the operation, irrespective of its length; post-discharge means days 1 to 90 after discharge; the complete endpoint combines the two. The duration analysis is a first-event piecewise Poisson model using observed person-time, comparing each of the first 18 weeks after discharge with a single late reference interval spanning post-discharge days 180 to 365.
Interpreting event timing: As median length of stay shortened, a larger proportion of recorded events occurred after discharge. The complete endpoint, comprising the whole admission for the operation and the first 90 days after discharge, is therefore the primary national trend outcome. Its components show where events were recorded within the care pathway. These are recorded event rates over time; the study does not estimate a causal effect of surgery, of any prophylaxis regimen, or of prophylaxis duration.

Postoperative risk over time: First-event piecewise Poisson models compare the event rate in each week after discharge with the rate in a single late reference interval spanning post-discharge days 180 to 365, using observed person-time. An incidence rate ratio above 1 indicates a higher event rate than the later reference rate. Follow-up ends at the first outcome being modelled, death, a subsequent hip or knee replacement, the administrative end of the linked data on 31 March 2025, or day 365; patients are not required to survive to the late reference interval. Pulmonary embolism and major bleeding each combine qualifying hospital records with qualifying linked deaths. Models are adjusted for age group, sex, Charlson Comorbidity Index, Index of Multiple Deprivation quintile, calendar period, and indicators for prior venous thromboembolism, major bleeding, hip replacement and knee replacement. The analysis beginning after discharge is primary. The timing sensitivity begins on the recorded arthroplasty procedure date, or on the start date of the procedure episode when the operation date is unavailable or unusable, and assigns diagnoses recorded in the procedure episode to procedure day 0.

Bleeding details: Post-discharge major bleeding events were grouped into six non-traumatic hospital-recorded subtypes, alongside a seventh category for qualifying linked deaths without a matching hospital record. Categories are not mutually exclusive, and this descriptive analysis does not identify the effect of any medication or prophylaxis regimen.

Subtype counts and proportions

Upper gastrointestinal bleeding and intracranial bleeding were identified from any diagnostic position. Lower gastrointestinal bleeding, haematuria, respiratory bleeding and other bleeding were identified from the primary diagnostic position. The intracranial bleeding phenotype includes I60, I61, I62.0, I62.1 and I62.9, and covers non-traumatic subdural haemorrhage as well as intracerebral and subarachnoid haemorrhage. ONS-only fatal major bleeding denotes a qualifying linked death within 90 days after discharge with a bleeding code as the underlying cause, where no matching hospital record was found in that window. Subtypes are not mutually exclusive, because a single bleeding admission can be coded with more than one subtype, so percentages sum to more than 100.
