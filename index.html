Pulmonary embolism and major bleeding during and after primary elective hip and knee replacement in England

National trends and postoperative risk, 2006 to 2024 | HES-APC linked to ONS death records

Question 1 How did pulmonary embolism and major bleeding change nationally from 2006 to 2024?
Question 2 How long did excess risk persist after discharge?
Study design: This population based retrospective cohort included adults undergoing primary elective total knee or hip replacement in England between 1 January 2006 and 30 September 2024. The national trend analysis reports events during the index admission and within 90 days after discharge, both together and separately. The self controlled crossover analysis compares each week after discharge with the corresponding week 180 days later in the same patient.
Interpreting event timing: As median length of stay shortened, a larger proportion of recorded events occurred after discharge. The complete endpoint, comprising the index admission and 90 days after discharge, is therefore the primary national trend outcome. Its components show where events were recorded within the care pathway.

Postoperative risk over time: Each 7 day risk interval was paired with the corresponding 7 day reference interval 180 days later in the same patient. An odds ratio above 1 indicates higher event odds in the postoperative interval. The post-discharge analysis is primary; the admission anchored analysis is a sensitivity analysis that also captures events during the index admission.
