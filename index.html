Pulmonary embolism and major bleeding during and after primary elective hip and knee replacement in England: national trends and postoperative risk, 2006 to 2024

Question 1 How did pulmonary embolism and major bleeding change nationally from 2006 to 2024?
Question 2 How long did excess risk persist after discharge?
Study design: This population based retrospective cohort included adults undergoing primary elective total knee or hip replacement in England between 1 January 2006 and 30 September 2024. The national trend analysis reports events during the index admission and within 90 days after discharge, both together and separately. The duration analysis is a first-event piecewise Poisson model using observed person-time, comparing each of the first 18 weeks after discharge with a single late reference interval spanning post-discharge days 180 to 365.
Interpreting event timing: As median length of stay shortened, a larger proportion of recorded events occurred after discharge. The complete endpoint, comprising the index admission and 90 days after discharge, is therefore the primary national trend outcome. Its components show where events were recorded within the care pathway.

Postoperative risk over time: First-event piecewise Poisson models compare the event rate in each week after discharge with the rate in a single late reference interval spanning post-discharge days 180 to 365, using observed person-time. An incidence rate ratio above 1 indicates a higher event rate than the late reference rate. Follow-up ends at the first outcome being modelled, death, a subsequent hip or knee replacement, the administrative end of the linked data on 31 March 2025, or day 365; patients are not required to survive to the late reference interval. Pulmonary embolism combines HES-recorded events with ONS pulmonary embolism-coded deaths. Models are adjusted for age group, sex, Charlson Comorbidity Index, Index of Multiple Deprivation quintile, calendar period, and distant-history indicators. The post-discharge analysis is primary; the admission anchored analysis is a sensitivity analysis that also includes events during the index admission.

Bleeding details: Post-discharge major bleeding events were grouped into six non-traumatic subtypes. Categories are not mutually exclusive, and this descriptive analysis does not identify the effect of any medication or prophylaxis regimen.

Subtype counts and proportions

Upper gastrointestinal bleeding and intracranial bleed were identified from any diagnostic position. Lower gastrointestinal bleeding, haematuria, respiratory bleeding and other bleeding were identified from the primary diagnostic position. The intracranial bleed phenotype includes I60, I61, I62.0, I62.1 and I62.9, and covers non-traumatic subdural haemorrhage as well as intracerebral and subarachnoid haemorrhage. Subtypes are not mutually exclusive, because a single bleeding admission can be coded with more than one subtype, so percentages sum to more than 100. Cells with fewer than 10 events are reported as <10 to comply with statistical disclosure control, and the underlying counts are withheld from this page rather than only hidden from display.
